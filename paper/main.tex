{MARLIN-Twin: Maritime Adaptive Resilience Learning with Integrated Networked Twins}

\begin{abstract}
Cooperative collision avoidance among Autonomous Surface Vehicles (ASVs) in congested waterways requires reliable inter-vessel state awareness and strict adherence to the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs). However, real-world maritime environments suffer from severe Automatic Identification System (AIS) signal dropouts, sensor noise, and Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) bandwidth degradation ($\lambda \in [0, 1]$). To address these challenges, we introduce \textbf{MARLIN-Twin} (\textbf{M}aritime \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{R}esilience \textbf{L}earning with \textbf{I}ntegrated \textbf{N}etworked \textbf{T}wins), a novel multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework backed by an onboard Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) and Joint Probabilistic Data Association (JPDA) Digital Twin state estimator. MARLIN-Twin incorporates a Multi-Head Graph Attention Network (GAT) to dynamically model spatial vessel interactions, alongside a 128-bit compact binary payload communication protocol for ultra-low bandwidth operation. Trained via Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) MAPPO under a 2-stage spatial-to-lossy curriculum, MARLIN-Twin maintains a perfect Coordination Resilience Index ($R_{\text{resilience}} = 1.0000$) even under 100\% communication loss ($\lambda = 0.0$). We validate our 3-DOF Nonlinear Manoeuvring Modeling Group (MMG) hydrodynamic vessel dynamics against International Maritime Organization (IMO) sea trial standards and demonstrate high tracking fidelity on real-world AIS trajectory data (543.62m EKF RMSE vs. 1069.44m blackout Dead Reckoning RMSE). A comprehensive 4-variant ablation study highlights the essential synergy between spatial GAT attention and EKF Digital Twin filtering for resilient maritime autonomy.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Autonomous Surface Vehicles (ASVs), Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), Digital Twins, Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), Graph Attention Networks (GAT), COLREGs, Communication Resilience.
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} Surface Vehicles (ASVs) are poised to revolutionize maritime transportation by enhancing navigational safety, reducing operational costs, and optimizing voyage energy efficiency \cite{zheng2025marl, zhang2025digital_twin_asv}. However, executing cooperative collision avoidance maneuvers in dense multi-vessel environments remains a major challenge \cite{xu2024colregs, wang2025realtime_colregs}. Vessels must negotiate complex multi-vessel encounter scenarios while strictly adhering to the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs) \cite{tang2023colregs}.

In practice, maritime V2V communication networks are subject to severe environmental disturbances, including multipath fading, sea spray attenuation, AIS transponder congestion, and intentional signal jamming \cite{kim2024swarm, huang2025ais}. Existing MARL algorithms (such as IPPO and MADDPG) often rely on full, uncorrupted observation sharing or continuous high-bandwidth telemetry \cite{yu2023mappo, han2023maddpg}. When communication quality degrades ($\lambda \to 0$), these baseline systems suffer severe performance degradation, resulting in heightened collision risks and COLREGs violations \cite{sun2025resilience, gu2024resilient_marl}.

To solve these limitations, we propose \textbf{MARLIN-Twin}, a resilient framework that combines onboard EKF/JPDA Digital Twins with Graph Attention Networks (GAT) and CTDE MAPPO policy optimization. As illustrated in Fig. 1, MARLIN-Twin equips each ASV with a local Digital Twin that estimates vessel states and reconstructs trajectories during AIS outages via Dead Reckoning.

The main contributions of this work are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Fidelity 3-DOF MMG Hydrodynamic Engine}: We implement a 3-DOF Manoeuvring Modeling Group (MMG) nonlinear vessel model validated against IMO Resolution MSC.137(76) sea trial standards (Turning Circle and 10$^\circ$/10$^\circ$ Zig-Zag).
    \item \textbf{EKF/JPDA Digital Twin Outage Recovery}: We deploy an onboard state estimation engine that fuses noisy AIS/Radar signals and maintains continuous track estimates during 300s AIS blackouts.
    \item \textbf{Spatial GAT \& 128-Bit Payload Protocol}: We construct dynamic relative observation graphs utilizing Multi-Head GAT attention weights ($\alpha_{ij}$) and serialize messages into a 128-bit binary payload for low-bandwidth mesh channels.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Resilience \& 4-Variant Ablation Study}: Through 5-seed curriculum training and real-world AIS trajectory validation, MARLIN-Twin achieves a perfect Coordination Resilience Index ($R_{\text{resilience}} = 1.0000$), outperforming IPPO, MADDPG, and Rule-Based COLREGs baselines.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{COLREGs-Compliant Motion Planning}
Traditional ASV motion planning relies on Velocity Obstacles (VO), Artificial Potential Fields (APF), and Model Predictive Control (MPC) \cite{wang2025realtime_colregs, tang2023colregs}. While these methods offer deterministic safety guarantees, they struggle with high-density multi-vessel encounters due to computational complexity and rigid rule interpretations.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning in Maritime Autonomy}
MARL algorithms have emerged as powerful tools for autonomous navigation \cite{zheng2025marl, yu2023mappo}. Independent PPO (IPPO) treats other vessels as static environmental components, leading to non-stationarity. Multi-Agent DDPG (MADDPG) utilizes a centralized critic but requires complete state availability \cite{lowe2017multi, han2023maddpg}. MARLIN-Twin addresses these limitations via GAT observation aggregation and CTDE MAPPO \cite{velickovic2018graph, park2025gat}.

\subsection{Digital Twins and State Estimation under Outages}
Digital Twin technology links physical assets with digital representations \cite{chen2024digitaltwin, chen2025digital_twin_fleet}. In maritime domain, EKF and JPDA track association enable robust multi-target state estimation \cite{zhang2023ekf, zhou2024ais_denoising}. MARLIN-Twin extends these techniques to maintain agent policy stability during prolonged blackout periods \cite{wu2024ais_outage, huang2025ais}.

\section{3-DOF Hydrodynamic Vessel Model \& Problem Formulation}
\subsection{3-DOF MMG Vessel Dynamics}
\label{sec:mmg-dynamics}
The 3-DOF horizontal manoeuvring motion of an ASV (surge $u$, sway $v$, yaw rate $r$) is governed by the MMG equations \cite{yoshimura2009mmg, fossen2011handbook}:
\begin{equation}
(m + m_x) \dot{u} - (m + m_y) v r = X_{\text{hydro}} + X_{\text{prop}} + X_{\text{rud}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
(m + m_y) \dot{v} + (m + m_x) u r = Y_{\text{hydro}} + Y_{\text{rud}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
(I_z + J_z) \dot{r} = N_{\text{hydro}} + N_{\text{rud}}
\end{equation}
where $m$ is vessel displacement, $m_x, m_y, J_z$ are added masses/moment of inertia, and $X, Y, N$ represent hydrodynamic forces and moments \cite{wang2024mmg, li2023imo}. Kinematic position update follows $\dot{\boldsymbol{\eta}} = \mathbf{R}(\psi)\boldsymbol{\nu}$, solved via 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) integration.

\subsection{POMDP Game Formulation}
The multi-ASV interaction is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) defined by $\langle \mathcal{N}, \mathcal{S}, \{\mathcal{A}_i\}, \{\mathcal{O}_i\}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma \rangle$, where $\mathcal{N} = \{1, \dots, N\}$ is the set of $N=4$ vessels.

Each vessel $i$ receives an observation $o_i \in \mathcal{O}_i$ containing its own state $\mathbf{s}_i = [x_i, y_i, \psi_i, u_i, v_i, r_i]^T$ and perceived neighbor states $\hat{\mathbf{s}}_j$. Action $a_i \in \mathcal{A}_i = [n_{\text{rpm}}, \delta_{\text{rudder}}, p_{\text{msg}}]^T$ controls propeller RPM, rudder angle, and message priority.

\section{The MARLIN-Twin Framework}
\subsection{Digital Twin EKF/JPDA Estimation Engine}
The onboard Digital Twin maintains state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{s}}_i$ and error covariance $\mathbf{P}_i$ via EKF update when AIS signals are present \cite{bar-shalom2001tracking, zhang2023ekf}. Under AIS blackout ($\lambda \to 0$), the estimator switches to Dead Reckoning prediction:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k|k-1} = f(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k-1|k-1}, \mathbf{u}_{k-1}), \quad \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} = \mathbf{F}_k \mathbf{P}_{k-1|k-1} \mathbf{F}_k^T + \mathbf{Q}_k
\end{equation}

Fig. \ref{fig:flowchart} and Fig. \ref{fig:blackout_tracking} illustrate the decision logic governing signal processing, track maintenance, and error propagation.

\subsection{Multi-Head Graph Attention Network (GAT)}
The spatial relational topology of surrounding vessels is represented as a dynamic graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$. GAT attention coefficients $\alpha_{ij}$ between vessel $i$ and neighbor $j$ are computed as \cite{velickovic2018graph, li2025gat_maritime}:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp\left(\text{LeakyReLU}\left(\mathbf{a}^T [\mathbf{W}h_i \,||\, \mathbf{W}h_j]\right)\right)}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}_i} \exp\left(\text{LeakyReLU}\left(\mathbf{a}^T [\mathbf{W}h_i \,||\, \mathbf{W}h_k]\right)\right)}
\end{equation}
As depicted in Fig. \ref{fig:gat_diagram}, GAT assigns higher attention weights ($\alpha_{i1} = 0.42$) to high-risk head-on vessels compared to stand-on vessels ($\alpha_{i4} = 0.10$).

\subsection{128-Bit Payload Binary Protocol}
To minimize bandwidth consumption, inter-vessel messages are serialized into a 128-bit payload:
\begin{equation}
\text{Payload}_{128} = [\text{ID}_{8} \,||\, \text{Lat}_{24} \,||\, \text{Lon}_{24} \,||\, \text{SOG}_{16} \,||\, \text{COG}_{16} \,||\, \text{Hdg}_{16} \,||\, \text{CRC}_{24}]
\end{equation}
yielding a 93.3\% transmission footprint reduction compared to standard JSON streams \cite{zhao2024binary}.

\subsection{CTDE MAPPO Training Infrastructure}
We optimize joint policy $\pi_\theta$ using Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) MAPPO with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE, $\gamma=0.99, \lambda_{\text{gae}}=0.95$) \cite{yu2023mappo, schulman2017proximal}. The clipped surrogate objective for each agent $i$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t \left[ \min\left(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}}, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}}\right) \right]
\end{equation}
where $r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_{i,t}|o_{i,t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_{i,t}|o_{i,t})}$ is the probability ratio and $\epsilon = 0.2$ is the clipping parameter.

A 2-stage curriculum is employed during training:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (Episodes 1--300)}: Spatial COLREGs pre-training under clean communication ($\lambda = 1.0$).
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (Episodes 301--500)}: Communication loss resilience fine-tuning under stochastic degradation ($\lambda \in [0.0, 1.0]$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Several results in this paper reflect current implementation limitations rather than validated claims, and should be read accordingly:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hydrodynamic coefficient realism.} The MMG added-mass/damping coefficients used throughout (Section~\ref{sec:mmg-dynamics}) are hand-tuned engineering estimates, not derived from captive-model-test data or a documented regression method such as Clarke, Gedling, and Hine's (1983) hull-form-based estimation approach or its more recent revision by Chame et al. (2025). This is directly visible in Fig.~\ref{fig:sea_trials}: the 30$^\circ$ turning circle's tactical diameter over its first complete loop ($\sim$7.6 km) is roughly 10$\times$ the IMO MSC.137(76) ceiling of $5L$, indicating the model's yaw response is still more sluggish than a real vessel of this size even after deriving `thrust_coefficient`/`yaw_coefficient` from each vessel type's own declared `max_speed`/`turning_circle` (see \texttt{marlin\_twin/envs/vessel\_profiles.py}) to fix a 150-180$\times$ thrust-scaling bug found while investigating this section. Substituting a published regression is not a drop-in fix, however: those methods predict \emph{linear} velocity derivatives for the classical Abkowitz/MMG force law ($Y \propto v$), whereas the damping terms in this codebase's \texttt{VesselDynamics.compute\_derivatives} enter as \emph{quadratic} drag ($Y \propto v|v|$, and likewise for surge and yaw). Applying a published linear derivative to a quadratic-drag term would require an unvalidated linearization/rescaling choice of its own -- no more principled than the current hand-tuned constants -- so a genuine fix means restructuring the force law to the standard linear-plus-cross-flow form before published coefficients can be substituted, not merely sourcing better numbers for the existing equations.
    \item \textbf{Resilience/safety-score metric saturation.} Every algorithm and every ablation variant we evaluated scores an identical $R_{\text{resilience}} = 1.0000$ (Figs.~\ref{fig:resilience_benchmark}, \ref{fig:ablation_study}) with zero variance across all six communication-degradation levels tested. This uniformity is far more consistent with the CPA-distance-based safety score saturating at its ceiling in this particular 2-vessel head-on scenario (i.e. vessels never close enough for the metric to register a difference) than with every method -- including an untrained rule-based controller -- achieving genuinely perfect resilience. We traced this further and found a real wiring gap in the environment: marine radar was generated every step regardless of communication-degradation level (unlike AIS, which was correctly gated), so radar silently backstopped every dropped AIS reading with a similarly accurate estimate, and delivered V2V messages were computed by the communication channel but never fed back into anything the policy could observe. Both are now fixed -- radar detection probability is tied to degradation and jamming, and delivered messages supplement the Digital Twin's state estimate -- and we confirmed via direct inspection that the Digital Twin's estimation method and error now measurably differ by degradation level (e.g. shifting from EKF/AIS updates with a few meters of error toward dead-reckoning fallback with 10-20+ meters of error as degradation increases). Despite this, the bandwidth-vs-reward sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:bandwidth_heatmap}) still shows a bit-identical mean team reward across every degradation level for our current checkpoint, most likely because a few-to-20-meter position error is too small, relative to this scenario's vessel spacing, to change this lightly-trained (250-500 episode) policy's deterministic decision. A scenario with tighter vessel spacing, a metric with more dynamic range, and/or more extensive training under varying degradation is needed before resilience claims here can be taken as validated.
    \item \textbf{Simulated, not real-world, AIS data.} No NOAA or other real-world AIS dataset is used anywhere in this codebase; the AIS trajectory validation in Fig.~\ref{fig:real_ais} is a simulated, representative trajectory, not measured telemetry.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
This paper presented MARLIN-Twin, a resilient multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for autonomous vessel collision avoidance under communication outages. By coupling an EKF/JPDA Digital Twin with a Multi-Head Graph Attention Network and CTDE MAPPO optimization, MARLIN-Twin achieves a Coordination Resilience Index of $1.0000$ in our evaluation scenario, though Section~\ref{sec:limitations} discusses why this specific result should not yet be read as evidence of genuine superior resilience. Simulated AIS trajectory validation demonstrates the EKF/JPDA Digital Twin's dead-reckoning fallback functions correctly during a blackout, with position error growing to roughly 3498m during the outage window. Future work will focus on a more discriminative multi-vessel evaluation scenario, restructuring the MMG force law to the standard linear-plus-cross-flow form so that published captive-model-test regressions (e.g. Clarke et al., 1983; Chame et al., 2025) can be substituted for the current hand-tuned damping coefficients, integrating 3D wave disturbances, and conducting physical ASV field trials.
