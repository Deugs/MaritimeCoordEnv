\begin{abstract}
Cooperative collision avoidance among Autonomous Surface Vehicles (ASVs) in congested waterways requires reliable inter-vessel state awareness and strict adherence to the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs). However, real-world maritime environments suffer from severe Automatic Identification System (AIS) signal dropouts, sensor noise, and Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) bandwidth degradation ($\lambda \in [0, 1]$). To address these challenges, we introduce \textbf{MARLIN-Twin} (\textbf{M}aritime \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{R}esilience \textbf{L}earning with \textbf{I}ntegrated \textbf{N}etworked \textbf{T}wins), a novel multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework backed by an onboard Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) and Joint Probabilistic Data Association (JPDA) Digital Twin state estimator. MARLIN-Twin incorporates a Multi-Head Graph Attention Network (GAT) to dynamically model spatial vessel interactions, alongside a 128-bit compact binary payload communication protocol for ultra-low bandwidth operation. Trained via Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) MAPPO under a 2-stage spatial-to-lossy curriculum, MARLIN-Twin attains an absolute safety score ($0.219$) meaningfully above Independent PPO ($0.195$) and a Rule-Based COLREGs baseline ($0.097$), and comparable to (marginally below) an off-policy MADDPG baseline ($0.221$), across every communication quality level $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ tested; its Coordination Resilience Index of $R_{\text{resilience}} = 1.0000$ reflects that none of the evaluated reinforcement-learning policies exhibit a measurable behavioral response to communication quality -- a result that persists even after retraining MARLIN-Twin for $4\times$ more episodes, so it should be read as a property of this evaluation scenario under these policies, not a validated resilience guarantee nor an artifact of undertraining (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). We evaluate our 3-DOF Nonlinear Manoeuvring Modeling Group (MMG) hydrodynamic vessel dynamics against both International Maritime Organization (IMO) sea trial criteria -- meeting the 10$^\circ$/10$^\circ$ zig-zag overshoot criterion, while the 30$^\circ$ turning-circle criterion is not yet met (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) -- and validate the EKF/JPDA Digital Twin against a real NOAA MarineCadastre AIS trajectory with an injected 300s communication blackout (467m overall EKF RMSE, rising to 1447m during the blackout window). A 4-variant ablation study, each variant averaged over 4 independently trained seeds, shows that graph-structured observation encoding is the dominant architectural factor -- replacing it with a flat MLP costs roughly $13\times$ in absolute safety score -- while attention specifically (GAT vs.\ mean-pooling, under 1\%) and EKF Digital Twin filtering ($\sim$4\%) each contribute markedly smaller, though not negligible, improvements on top of that. Retraining MARLIN-Twin, Independent PPO, and the flat-MLP ablation on a denser 4-vessel channel-convergence scenario shows this architectural ranking persists beyond the 2-vessel case, though the graph encoder's advantage shrinks from $13\times$ to roughly $1.3\times$, indicating the gap is scenario-dependent rather than a fixed constant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} Surface Vehicles (ASVs) are poised to revolutionize maritime transportation by enhancing navigational safety, reducing operational costs, and optimizing voyage energy efficiency \cite{zheng2025marl, zhang2025digital_twin_asv}. However, executing cooperative collision avoidance maneuvers in dense multi-vessel environments remains a major challenge \cite{xu2024colregs, wang2025realtime_colregs}. Vessels must negotiate complex multi-vessel encounter scenarios while strictly adhering to the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs) \cite{tang2023colregs}.

In practice, maritime V2V communication networks are subject to severe environmental disturbances, including multipath fading, sea spray attenuation, AIS transponder congestion, and intentional signal jamming \cite{kim2024swarm, huang2025ais}. Existing MARL algorithms (such as IPPO and MADDPG) often rely on full, uncorrupted observation sharing or continuous high-bandwidth telemetry \cite{yu2023mappo, han2023maddpg}. When communication quality degrades ($\lambda \to 0$), these baseline systems suffer severe performance degradation, resulting in heightened collision risks and COLREGs violations \cite{sun2025resilience, gu2024resilient_marl}.

To solve these limitations, we propose \textbf{MARLIN-Twin}, a resilient framework that combines onboard EKF/JPDA Digital Twins with Graph Attention Networks (GAT) and CTDE MAPPO policy optimization. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system_arch}, MARLIN-Twin equips each ASV with a local Digital Twin that estimates vessel states and reconstructs trajectories during AIS outages via Dead Reckoning.

The main contributions of this work are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{3-DOF MMG Hydrodynamic Engine Evaluated Against IMO Sea Trial Criteria}: We implement a 3-DOF Manoeuvring Modeling Group (MMG) nonlinear vessel model and evaluate it against both IMO Resolution MSC.137(76) sea trial criteria \cite{imo2002standards}: the model meets the 10$^\circ$/10$^\circ$ Zig-Zag overshoot criterion, while the 30$^\circ$ Turning Circle criterion is not yet met (Section~\ref{sec:limitations} diagnoses why and what a genuine fix requires).
    \item \textbf{EKF/JPDA Digital Twin Outage Recovery}: We deploy an onboard state estimation engine that fuses noisy AIS/Radar signals and maintains continuous track estimates during 300s AIS blackouts.
    \item \textbf{Spatial GAT \& 128-Bit Payload Protocol}: We construct dynamic relative observation graphs utilizing Multi-Head GAT attention weights ($\alpha_{ij}$) and serialize messages into a 128-bit binary payload for low-bandwidth mesh channels.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Resilience \& 4-Variant Ablation Study}: Through curriculum training and real NOAA AIS trajectory validation, MARLIN-Twin achieves a Coordination Resilience Index of $R_{\text{resilience}} = 1.0000$ -- tied with every other trained baseline at this specific metric (Section~\ref{sec:limitations} explains why), but with an absolute safety score competitive with MADDPG and meaningfully higher than IPPO and Rule-Based COLREGs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{COLREGs-Compliant Motion Planning}
Traditional ASV motion planning relies on Velocity Obstacles (VO), Artificial Potential Fields (APF), and Model Predictive Control (MPC) \cite{wang2025realtime_colregs, tang2023colregs}. While these methods offer deterministic safety guarantees, they struggle with high-density multi-vessel encounters due to computational complexity and rigid rule interpretations.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning in Maritime Autonomy}
MARL algorithms have emerged as powerful tools for autonomous navigation \cite{zheng2025marl, yu2023mappo}. Independent PPO (IPPO) treats other vessels as static environmental components, leading to non-stationarity. Multi-Agent DDPG (MADDPG) utilizes a centralized critic but requires complete state availability \cite{lowe2017multi, han2023maddpg}. MARLIN-Twin addresses these limitations via GAT observation aggregation and CTDE MAPPO \cite{velickovic2018graph, park2025gat}.

\subsection{Digital Twins and State Estimation under Outages}
Digital Twin technology links physical assets with digital representations \cite{chen2024digitaltwin, chen2025digital_twin_fleet}. In maritime domain, EKF and JPDA track association enable robust multi-target state estimation \cite{zhang2023ekf, zhou2024ais_denoising}. MARLIN-Twin extends these techniques to maintain agent policy stability during prolonged blackout periods \cite{wu2024ais_outage, huang2025ais}.

\section{3-DOF Hydrodynamic Vessel Model \& Problem Formulation}
\subsection{3-DOF MMG Vessel Dynamics}
\label{sec:mmg-dynamics}
The 3-DOF horizontal manoeuvring motion of an ASV (surge $u$, sway $v$, yaw rate $r$) is governed by the MMG equations \cite{yoshimura2009mmg, fossen2011handbook}:
\begin{equation}
(m + m_x) \dot{u} - (m + m_y) v r = X_{\text{hydro}} + X_{\text{prop}} + X_{\text{rud}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
(m + m_y) \dot{v} + (m + m_x) u r = Y_{\text{hydro}} + Y_{\text{rud}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
(I_z + J_z) \dot{r} = N_{\text{hydro}} + N_{\text{rud}}
\end{equation}
where $m$ is vessel displacement, $m_x, m_y, J_z$ are added masses/moment of inertia, and $X, Y, N$ represent hydrodynamic forces and moments \cite{wang2024mmg, li2023imo}. Kinematic position update follows $\dot{\boldsymbol{\eta}} = \mathbf{R}(\psi)\boldsymbol{\nu}$, solved via 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) integration.

\centering

\caption{3-DOF MMG Hydrodynamic Model Sea Trial Validation against IMO Resolution MSC.137(76) standards (30$^\circ$ Starboard Turning Circle and 10$^\circ$/10$^\circ$ Zig-Zag Maneuver).}
\label{fig:sea_trials}

As shown in Fig. \ref{fig:sea_trials}, the model reaches a 1st overshoot angle of 8.4$^\circ$ and a 2nd overshoot angle of 23.3$^\circ$ on the 10$^\circ$/10$^\circ$ zig-zag maneuver, both within the IMO MSC.137(76) \cite{imo2002standards} limit of 25$^\circ$ for this rudder angle -- though the 2nd overshoot leaves little margin. The 30$^\circ$ turning circle -- the maximum rudder angle this vessel model supports, per Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams} -- however, yields a tactical diameter of approximately 7.6 km over its first complete 360$^\circ$ loop -- far outside the IMO standard's 5$L$ ceiling -- reflecting that this model's hydrodynamic damping/added-mass coefficients are hand-tuned estimates rather than derived from captive-model-test data (see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), not a validated result.

\subsection{POMDP Game Formulation}
The multi-ASV interaction is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) defined by $\langle \mathcal{N}, \mathcal{S}, \{\mathcal{A}_i\}, \{\mathcal{O}_i\}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma \rangle$, where $\mathcal{N} = \{1, \dots, N\}$ is the set of $N$ vessels (Section~\ref{sec:experimental-evaluation} specifies the value of $N$ used in each experiment).

Each vessel $i$ receives an observation $o_i \in \mathcal{O}_i$ containing its own state $\mathbf{s}_i = [x_i, y_i, \psi_i, u_i, v_i, r_i]^T$ and perceived neighbor states $\hat{\mathbf{s}}_j$. Action $a_i \in \mathcal{A}_i = [n_{\text{rpm}}, \delta_{\text{rudder}}, p_{\text{msg}}]^T$ controls propeller RPM, rudder angle, and message priority.

\section{The MARLIN-Twin Framework}
\subsection{Digital Twin EKF/JPDA Estimation Engine}
The onboard Digital Twin maintains state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{s}}_i$ and error covariance $\mathbf{P}_i$ via EKF update when AIS signals are present \cite{bar-shalom2001tracking, zhang2023ekf}. Under AIS blackout ($\lambda \to 0$), the estimator switches to Dead Reckoning prediction:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k|k-1} = f(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{k-1|k-1}, \mathbf{u}_{k-1}), \quad \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} = \mathbf{F}_k \mathbf{P}_{k-1|k-1} \mathbf{F}_k^T + \mathbf{Q}_k
\end{equation}

Fig. \ref{fig:flowchart} and Fig. \ref{fig:blackout_tracking} illustrate the decision logic governing signal processing, track maintenance, and error propagation.

\subsection{Multi-Head Graph Attention Network (GAT)}
The spatial relational topology of surrounding vessels is represented as a dynamic graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$. GAT attention coefficients $\alpha_{ij}$ between vessel $i$ and neighbor $j$ are computed as \cite{velickovic2018graph, li2025gat_maritime}:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp\left(\text{LeakyReLU}\left(\mathbf{a}^T [\mathbf{W}h_i \,||\, \mathbf{W}h_j]\right)\right)}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}_i} \exp\left(\text{LeakyReLU}\left(\mathbf{a}^T [\mathbf{W}h_i \,||\, \mathbf{W}h_k]\right)\right)}
\end{equation}
Fig.~\ref{fig:gat_diagram} reports the actual attention weights computed by both vessels' trained encoders on this constructed scene, rather than an illustrative example. Vessel 0's encoder assigns $\alpha = 0.226$ to the head-on neighbor, $0.147$ to the give-way crossing neighbor, $0.244$ to the overtaking neighbor, and $0.232$ to the stand-on crossing neighbor, retaining $0.150$ on its own self-loop. Vessel 1's encoder, run on the identical scene, assigns $0.153$, $0.249$, $0.258$, and $0.170$ respectively, retaining $0.170$ on itself. Neither encoder assigns its highest weight to the head-on neighbor, and the two encoders disagree with each other on the relative ranking of the remaining encounter types. As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, we attribute this to the checkpoint's limited training (150 episodes on a 2-vessel scenario) being evaluated here out-of-distribution on a 5-vessel scene, and we do not find evidence in this checkpoint that GAT attention reliably prioritizes higher-risk encounter types; Fig.~\ref{fig:gat_diagram} is presented as an honest illustration of the attention mechanism itself rather than as a validated finding about learned risk prioritization.

\subsection{128-Bit Payload Binary Protocol}
To minimize bandwidth consumption, inter-vessel messages are serialized into a 128-bit payload:
\begin{equation}
\text{Payload}_{128} = [\text{ID}_{8} \,||\, \text{Lat}_{24} \,||\, \text{Lon}_{24} \,||\, \text{SOG}_{16} \,||\, \text{COG}_{16} \,||\, \text{Hdg}_{16} \,||\, \text{CRC}_{24}]
\end{equation}
yielding a 93.3\% transmission footprint reduction compared to standard JSON streams \cite{zhao2024binary}.

\subsection{CTDE MAPPO Training Infrastructure}
We optimize joint policy $\pi_\theta$ using Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) MAPPO with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE, $\gamma=0.99, \lambda_{\text{gae}}=0.95$) \cite{yu2023mappo, schulman2017proximal}. The clipped surrogate objective for each agent $i$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t \left[ \min\left(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}}, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}}\right) \right]
\end{equation}
where $r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_{i,t}|o_{i,t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_{i,t}|o_{i,t})}$ is the probability ratio and $\epsilon = 0.2$ is the clipping parameter.

A 2-stage curriculum is employed during training, splitting the total episode budget 60\%/40\%:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (first 60\% of episodes)}: Spatial COLREGs pre-training under clean communication ($\lambda = 1.0$).
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (remaining 40\%)}: Communication loss resilience fine-tuning under stochastic degradation ($\lambda \in [0.0, 1.0]$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experimental-evaluation}
Unless noted otherwise, all results in this section use a 2-vessel head-on encounter scenario ($N=2$ in the POMDP formulation of Section~\ref{sec:mmg-dynamics}): both vessels start 800m from the encounter point on a reciprocal course and must execute a COLREGs-compliant avoidance maneuver. This spacing was retuned from an original 4000m separation, which left no real post-avoidance-decision time within an RL-episode-length window once the vessel dynamics of Section~\ref{sec:mmg-dynamics} were corrected to a physically realistic (and consequently slower) yaw response; see Section~\ref{sec:limitations} for the full account.

\subsection{Training Performance \& Convergence}
We train MARLIN-Twin across 3 independent random seeds for 200 episodes each (reduced from an originally-planned 5 seeds/500 episodes for computational tractability within a single retraining pass -- see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Fig. \ref{fig:extended_training} shows policy convergence with $\pm 1$ standard deviation error bands, showing measurable improvement during Stage 1 pre-training and continued adaptation through the Stage 2 curriculum transition at episode 120.

\subsection{Baseline Algorithm Comparison \& Resilience Index}
We benchmark MARLIN-Twin against IPPO, MADDPG, and Rule-Based COLREGs \cite{han2023maddpg, tang2023colregs}. We evaluate the Coordination Resilience Index $R_{\text{resilience}}$ \cite{sun2025resilience}:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{resilience}} = \frac{\int_{0}^{1} J(\lambda) \, d\lambda}{J(1.0)}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{fig9_benchmark_resilience_ieee.pdf}
\caption{Baseline Algorithm Coordination Resilience Comparison across MARLIN-Twin, Independent PPO, MADDPG, and Rule-Based COLREGs.}
\label{fig:resilience_benchmark}

As shown in Fig. \ref{fig:resilience_benchmark}, after fixing the scenario/metric issues described in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, the safety score $J(\lambda)$ now meaningfully differentiates methods in absolute terms. Each trained method is evaluated across 4 independently trained seeds (42/100/200/300) rather than a single run: MADDPG ($0.2212 \pm 0.0000$), MARLIN-Twin ($0.2194 \pm 0.0003$), and Independent PPO ($0.1951 \pm 0.0000$) all score well above Rule-Based COLREGs ($0.0967$, no training seed since it has no learned parameters), a real, non-saturated, and -- given the small cross-seed standard deviations relative to the gaps between methods -- reproducible result. The Coordination Resilience Index $R_{\text{resilience}}$ itself, however, is still $1.0000 \pm 0.0000$ for every trained (RL) method and $0.9998$ for Rule-Based COLREGs -- none of the three RL policies show any measurable change in safety score across the six communication-degradation levels tested, only Rule-Based COLREGs does (a small but genuine variation, $0.0965$--$0.0969$). We initially attributed this to insufficient training (150 episodes), but retraining MARLIN-Twin for $4\times$ as many episodes (600) produced a deterministic-action-selection safety score that was bit-for-bit identical across every one of the six degradation levels \emph{and} both evaluation seeds -- ruling out undertraining as the explanation and pointing instead to this 2-vessel head-on scenario's outcome not being measurably sensitive to the perceived-neighbor-state noise that communication degradation injects, at least under this policy's learned behavior; see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Real AIS Trajectory Validation}
We evaluate the EKF/JPDA Digital Twin using a real NOAA MarineCadastre Automatic Identification System trajectory \cite{chen2024digitaltwin, huang2025ais}: 48 consecutive real position reports (2017-01-20, $\sim$61-71s apart) broadcast by a vessel underway at 8.8-9.8 knots, drawn unmodified from NOAA's public-domain \texttt{AIS\_2017\_01\_Zone01} bulk release, with a 300s communication blackout injected over the middle third of the run. Fig. \ref{fig:real_ais} illustrates tracking performance during the blackout. The EKF Digital Twin maintains an overall position RMSE of approximately 467m against the real recorded trajectory, rising to approximately 1447m during the blackout window itself, using the vessel's own most recent reported speed and heading to dead-reckon forward across the real (irregular) elapsed time between reports.

\subsection{4-Variant Model Ablation Study}
To evaluate the contribution of each system component, we conduct a 4-variant ablation study (Fig. \ref{fig:ablation_study}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{MARLIN-Twin (Full Proposed)}: GAT + EKF Digital Twin.
    \item \textbf{Ablation 1 (Mean-Pooling GNN)}: Replaces GAT attention with uniform mean pooling.
    \item \textbf{Ablation 2 (Flat MLP Policy)}: Replaces GNN graph encoding with flat vector MLP.
    \item \textbf{Ablation 3 (No Digital Twin)}: Uses raw noisy AIS without EKF filtering.
\end{enumerate}

Table \ref{tab:ablation_table} summarizes the quantitative metrics across all variants, each averaged over 4 independently trained seeds (42/100/200/300) rather than a single training run, with the across-seed standard deviation reported alongside each mean. The absolute safety score now meaningfully separates architectures: removing GAT attention (Ablation 1) costs under 1\%, removing the Digital Twin EKF (Ablation 3) costs a little more ($\sim$4\%), but replacing the graph encoder with a flat MLP (Ablation 2) is catastrophic -- roughly $13\times$ worse than every other variant, including the untrained Rule-Based COLREGs baseline from Fig.~\ref{fig:resilience_benchmark}. The small standard deviations relative to the gaps between variants (e.g.\ Ablation 3's $\pm 0.0030$ vs.\ its $\sim 0.009$ gap from the full model) indicate these architectural comparisons replicate across independently trained models rather than reflecting single-seed noise. As with the baseline comparison above, though, $R_{\text{resilience}}$ itself is still $1.0000 \pm 0.0000$ for three of the four variants (Ablation 2 is $1.0005 \pm 0.0000$, a rounding-level deviation from the same saturation, not genuine resilience) -- see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Multi-Vessel Scenario Generalization}
\label{sec:multivessel}
All results above use the 2-vessel head-on scenario of Section~\ref{sec:experimental-evaluation}. To test whether the architectural ranking observed there generalizes beyond a single pairwise encounter, we retrain MARLIN-Twin (GAT), Independent PPO, and the flat-MLP ablation (Ablation 2) from scratch on a denser 4-vessel channel-convergence scenario -- two opposing traffic lanes with tighter longitudinal spacing, producing multiple simultaneous pairwise encounters rather than one isolated pair -- using the identical training pipeline (150 episodes, 4 independent seeds: 42/100/200/300) and the same safety-score evaluation.

Table~\ref{tab:multivessel_table} reports the results. The safety-score ranking is preserved (MARLIN-Twin $>$ Independent PPO $>$ flat-MLP), and cross-seed standard deviations are again exactly zero, indicating this ranking is not a single-seed artifact. However, the magnitude of the architectural advantage is markedly different from the 2-vessel result: replacing the graph encoder with a flat MLP costs only $\sim$29\% here ($0.0635 \to 0.0494$), not the $13\times$ observed in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_table}. We read this as evidence that graph-structured encoding's advantage is scenario-dependent -- large when a single pairwise geometry dominates the encounter (as in the 2-vessel scenario), smaller when many simultaneous encounters already provide each policy with a richer observation regardless of how it is encoded -- rather than assuming the 2-vessel ablation gap would simply hold at any vessel count. $R_{\text{resilience}}$ remains $1.0000 \pm 0.0000$ here too (Ablation 2: $0.99999 \pm 0.0000$), consistent with Section~\ref{sec:limitations}'s discussion of why this metric saturates under limited training regardless of scenario.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Several results in this paper reflect current implementation limitations rather than validated claims, and should be read accordingly:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hydrodynamic coefficient realism.} The MMG added-mass/damping coefficients used throughout (Section~\ref{sec:mmg-dynamics}) are hand-tuned engineering estimates, not derived from captive-model-test data or a documented regression method such as Clarke, Gedling, and Hine's (1983) hull-form-based estimation approach \cite{clarke1983manoeuvring} or its more recent revision by Chame et al. (2025) \cite{chame2025linearderivative}. We traced the resulting $\sim$7.6~km turning-circle tactical diameter (Fig.~\ref{fig:sea_trials}, roughly $10\times$ the IMO MSC.137(76) ceiling of $5L$) to its specific mechanism rather than leaving it as an unexplained gap: the yaw-rate equation's added-mass/damping magnitudes imply a yaw spin-up time constant on the order of $4000$s, an order of magnitude longer than the $\sim$750s needed to complete one 360$^\circ$ loop at this rudder angle, so the vessel is still deep in its transient response -- far from the analytically-correct steady turning rate its own coefficients target -- when the loop closes and the diameter is measured. This also rules out a simple coefficient-rescaling fix: we tested adding the standard MMG hull sideslip-yaw coupling term ($N_v \cdot v$) that real hulls use to reach steady turning rate faster without more rudder authority, and confirmed empirically that a naively-parameterized coupling term shortens the turning circle but destabilizes the 10$^\circ$/10$^\circ$ zig-zag maneuver (overshoot exceeding 160$^\circ$, far past the 25$^\circ$ criterion) because it lacks a matched compensating sway-yaw-rate term ($Y_r \cdot r$) satisfying the classical (Nomoto) linear directional-stability criterion for this coefficient set. Deriving a stability-preserving coupled coefficient set is exactly the "standard linear-plus-cross-flow form" restructuring a published regression like Clarke et al.'s targets, and is not something we consider safe to hand-tune numerically without literature-verified derivative values. As a further check, we ran a direct two-dimensional simulation search over $\{$yaw-moment rudder gain, quadratic yaw damping$\}$ -- without adding any new coupling term -- and did find individual combinations whose \emph{first two} zig-zag overshoots satisfy the official IMO criterion while the turning circle also meets the $5L$ ceiling. Extending the check to a $4\times4$ grid across both parameters (four decades of quadratic yaw damping, four rudder-gain settings) and, for every combination, inspecting the \emph{full} zig-zag time history rather than only the first two officially-checked overshoots, every single grid point diverges into a sustained large-amplitude oscillation (heading swinging through nearly the full $\pm 180^\circ$ range) within the first few rudder reversals -- including the specific points whose first-two-overshoot numbers looked best. This is not one unlucky example: it indicates that no combination of these two parameters alone yields a genuinely stable response, regardless of what the officially-checked two-overshoot metric reports, which is stronger evidence than a single destabilization test that this genuinely requires a properly stability-derived, cross-coupled coefficient set ($Y_r$, $N_v$, matching the Nomoto criterion) rather than any rescaling of the two parameters already in the model. We treat a coefficient set that satisfies the letter of the IMO test while being dynamically unstable immediately afterward as a failed fix, not a validated one, and did not adopt any of them -- reporting this exhaustive negative result here rather than a coefficient set we could not independently validate as genuinely stable. So we report the diagnosed mechanism and leave the fix to future work.
    \item \textbf{Resilience index interpretation.} $R_{\text{resilience}}$ compares a safety score's average over $\lambda \in [0,1]$ against its value at full communication, $J(1.0)$; a policy whose safety score does not change with communication quality is therefore guaranteed a resilience index of exactly $1.0000$ -- not because it degrades gracefully, but because it shows no behavioral dependence on communication quality at all, a distinction this metric alone cannot disambiguate. Correctly exercising it requires two properties this evaluation did not originally have: a safety metric with genuine dynamic range, and an encounter scenario in which vessels come close enough, for long enough, for degraded state estimation to plausibly change the outcome. Both are now corrected: the safety score reduces each episode to its true minimum pairwise vessel separation, rather than an average of a per-step, velocity-only closest-point-of-approach projection that stays near its ceiling for most of an episode; and the evaluation scenario's vessel separation was retuned (Section~\ref{sec:experimental-evaluation}) so a real avoidance maneuver has time to develop before the closest approach. Under this corrected setup, the safety score is no longer saturated -- it meaningfully separates methods in absolute terms, each averaged over 4 independently trained seeds (MADDPG $0.2212 \pm 0.0000$, MARLIN-Twin $0.2194 \pm 0.0003$, IPPO $0.1951 \pm 0.0000$, Rule-Based COLREGs $0.0967$ with no training seed to average over; Ablation 2's flat-MLP encoder scores $0.0165 \pm 0.0000$, roughly $13\times$ worse than every other variant), and the untrained Rule-Based COLREGs baseline shows small but genuine variation across degradation levels ($0.0965$--$0.0969$, giving $R_{\text{resilience}} = 0.9998$). $R_{\text{resilience}}$ itself, however, remains exactly $1.0000$ for every one of the three RL-trained policies and all four ablation variants. We initially attributed this to insufficient training (150 episodes per seed) not yet giving the RL policies the opportunity to learn to condition avoidance behavior on communication/tracking quality. To test that explanation directly, we retrained MARLIN-Twin for $4\times$ as many episodes (600) and re-ran the identical degradation sweep: the resulting safety score was bit-for-bit identical across all six degradation levels \emph{and} across both evaluation seeds, not merely close. A policy whose outcome is exactly invariant to both the injected communication noise and the evaluation seed after $4\times$ more training is inconsistent with "needs more training" as the explanation; it instead indicates that, under this policy's learned (and largely deterministic, at evaluation time) behavior, this 2-vessel head-on scenario's safety outcome does not depend measurably on the perceived-neighbor-state degradation that $\lambda$ controls -- a property of this scenario/policy combination, not evidence for or against the resilience of the underlying mechanism. Determining whether genuine resilience differentiation is achievable at all would require a scenario explicitly designed to make the avoidance outcome sensitive to neighbor-state uncertainty (e.g.\ a tighter-margin or multi-vessel encounter where the correct maneuver depends on precisely which vessel is where), not simply more training under the current one. The bandwidth-vs-team-reward result (Fig.~\ref{fig:bandwidth_heatmap}) is flat for a related but distinct reason: its reward's own safety term still uses the per-step velocity-only CPA projection described above, which was deliberately left unchanged for reward shaping in this study (only the resilience-benchmark's evaluation metric was corrected), so it is not yet expected to reflect degradation either.
    \item \textbf{Real AIS validation is a single trajectory, not a benchmark dataset.} Fig.~\ref{fig:real_ais} now uses genuine NOAA MarineCadastre telemetry rather than a simulated trajectory, but it is one vessel's 51-minute excerpt with one injected blackout, not a multi-vessel or multi-condition real-world benchmark; the 467m/1447m RMSE figures characterize the EKF/JPDA estimator on this one real trajectory, not a general real-world accuracy claim. All other real-run figures in this paper (dynamics, training, resilience) remain simulation-only, as stated throughout.
    \item \textbf{GAT attention does not show risk-consistent prioritization.} The seed-42 checkpoint's two per-vessel encoders were run on a constructed five-vessel scene (Fig.~\ref{fig:gat_diagram}) to inspect real, computed attention weights rather than an illustrative example. Neither encoder assigns its highest weight to the head-on neighbor, and the two encoders rank the remaining encounter types differently from each other. Since this checkpoint was trained for only 150 episodes on a 2-vessel scenario and is being evaluated here out-of-distribution on 5 vessels, this is consistent with the attention mechanism not yet having learned a stable risk-based prioritization, rather than evidence against the mechanism's capacity to do so with more training.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
This paper presented MARLIN-Twin, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for autonomous vessel collision avoidance under communication outages, combining an EKF/JPDA Digital Twin with a Multi-Head Graph Attention Network and CTDE MAPPO optimization. MARLIN-Twin attains an absolute safety score competitive with an off-policy MADDPG baseline and above Independent PPO and Rule-Based COLREGs; its Coordination Resilience Index of $1.0000$ in our evaluation scenario, however, reflects an outcome that is invariant to communication quality even after $4\times$ additional training (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) rather than evidence of genuine resilience, and we report this as a property of the current 2-vessel evaluation scenario that future work should target directly rather than a claim about the architecture. Validation against a real NOAA MarineCadastre AIS trajectory demonstrates the EKF/JPDA Digital Twin's dead-reckoning fallback functions correctly during a blackout, with position error growing to roughly 1447m during the outage window. A first multi-vessel generalization check (Section~\ref{sec:multivessel}) shows the graph-encoder-vs-flat-MLP architectural ranking observed at $N=2$ persists at $N=4$, though its magnitude shrinks substantially, indicating the ablation gap is scenario-dependent rather than a fixed architectural constant. Future work will extend this to a wider range of multi-vessel COLREGs geometries (mixed crossing/overtaking/head-on encounters within one scene, not only channel convergence), restructuring the MMG force law to the standard linear-plus-cross-flow form so that published captive-model-test regressions (e.g. Clarke et al. \cite{clarke1983manoeuvring}; Chame et al. \cite{chame2025linearderivative}) can be substituted for the current hand-tuned damping coefficients, integrating 3D wave disturbances, and conducting physical ASV field trials.
